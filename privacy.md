# Kerja — Privacy Policy

## Who we are

Kerja is a professional networking app for digital nomads, starting in Canggu, Bali. This policy explains what personal data the Kerja app and the website trykerja.com collect, why, who receives it, and your rights.

Kerja is run by Zayd Benhacine, an independent developer (“Kerja”, “we”). We are responsible for your personal data. Contact us at hello@trykerja.com.

Last updated: 28 October 2026.

## The short version

- **We never store or send your position.** Location is used on the phone only, to centre the map, show distances to cafés, suggest the area you’re in, and check that you’re at a café when you start working there or arrive for a session. When the map shows your area, Mapbox delivers the map images for it.
- **Your profile is for members.** Signed-in members can see your profile. People you’ve blocked, and people who blocked you, can’t.
- **Check-ins and sessions are café-level; areas are neighbourhood-level.** When you start working at a café, or arrive for a session you joined, other members see you at that café until the end you chose or the session’s end, for 8 hours at most and never past closing time. They see when you’ll leave, never when you arrived. If you say you’re around an area today (for example Berawa), members see you there until midnight in Bali, and we delete it at 00:05. We delete each check-in 90 days after it ends, and each session with its seats 90 days after it ends.
- **Requests you send are stored** so the other person can answer: the reason, your optional note and, if you choose, a café to meet at. You choose who can send you requests.
- **Chats are only between the two of you.** Once a request is accepted, you can message each other. Nobody else sees your messages, unless one of you reports a message to us. Your messages show as “Sent”, then “Delivered”. Kerja shows when you’ve read a message only if you both turn on “Show when I’ve read messages”, and never shows when you’re typing or whether you’re online.
- **To keep Kerja safe,** our server refuses certain words, and we keep reports and bans as described below, even after an account is deleted.
- **Notifications never contain what people write to you.** They only say who did what, for example “New message from Maya” or “Maya joined your session at Crate Café”. They’re off until you turn them on.
- **The beta form on trykerja.com** keeps what you type in it, with a few details about it, for 6 months, and sends you one email to say we got it. You can ask us to delete it sooner.
- **trykerja.com counts visits, without cookies.** Through Umami, it counts the pages opened and a few taps, with the country, region and city worked out from the IP address, and keeps the counts for 6 months. The counts never hold your IP address, your name or anything you type in the form. You can turn counting off on the website’s Our promise page.
- **No selling, no ads, no tracking** across other apps or websites.
- **The app’s crash reports and statistics aren’t linked to your account:** no IP address, no location, no account ID.
- **You can delete your account in the app** (Me → Settings → Delete account). It removes your data from our database and photo storage at once, including every message you sent and the sessions you hosted. The people you chatted with keep only their own messages. Reports and bans are kept for safety, as described below.

**Location.** Kerja uses your location only while the app is open, and only if you allow it. The first time you open the map, Kerja explains why it asks; your phone’s own permission question follows only if you tap “Turn on location”. Your position stays in the phone’s memory: it centres the map, places your dot, gives distances to cafés and areas, suggests the nearest area, and checks that you’re at the café when you tap “Start working” or “I’m here” (Check in, in older versions of the app). For that check, Kerja reads your position once, or uses a reading the phone took in the last 2 minutes if it’s accurate to 100 metres, and checks that you’re within about 300 metres of the café, allowing for the reading’s own stated margin of error up to 100 metres; it needs precise location to be on. A recent reading can only let you in: if it doesn’t, the phone waits up to 10 seconds for a new one. Kerja never stores your position, its accuracy or your distance to the café, and never sends them to us or anyone else. “Start working” sends only the café, the end, the type, the number of seats and whether others can join, and our server sets the start; “I’m here”, and “Start working” where you already hold a seat there, send only the session. If you’re too far away, your phone tells you how far, and nothing is sent. Saying you’re around an area sends only the area’s name and, if you choose, what you’re up for; it doesn’t read your position, and neither does checking out. When the map shows the area around you, Mapbox delivers the map images for that area; it sees the area, not your position.

**Your photo.** Kerja opens your phone’s own photo picker, so it sees only the photo you choose, never the rest of your library. Before uploading, the phone crops it square and saves two new, smaller images: a small one for the map and lists, and a larger one, up to 1440 pixels wide, for the top of your profile. Making new images removes the photo’s hidden details, such as where and when it was taken and which camera took it. Photos are stored privately, and only your own account can open yours directly. Other members’ photos reach your phone through Kerja’s server, which checks each time that you’re allowed to see the photo and sends only the image, never when it was uploaded or changed. Photos never go to people on either side of a block, or to a banned or deleted account. Like any app, Kerja keeps a temporary copy of the photos it shows in the phone’s cache, and clears it when you sign out. Kerja uses the camera only when you tap “Take a photo” to take your profile photo, and only to take that photo; a photo taken with the camera is handled like one from your library. Kerja doesn’t use your microphone.

**Suggestions.** “People to meet today”, the good fits Kerja highlights, and the order of the people listed at a café are worked out on your phone, from profiles members can already see. Nothing about them is sent to us. So it doesn’t repeat them, your phone remembers the last 7 days’ suggestions (member IDs and dates), with your own roles and what you’re looking for when they were made, and deletes them when you sign out or delete your account. A profile can also offer the next of the day’s people to meet, from the same suggestions. “Things in common” on a profile is worked out on your phone from what both profiles show and whether you’ve worked together; nothing about it is stored or sent. On a profile, Kerja may suggest a first line for your Say hi, written on your phone from that member’s profile and your own role (for example “I’m a designer.”): it goes into your note, which you can change, and it’s sent only as your note when you tap Send. Nothing about it is stored, logged or sent before that.

**Search.** Searching for people, by name, role or what they can help with, is done on your phone. Your last 5 searches on the map and your last 5 in People stay on your phone, and are deleted when you sign out or delete your account.

**Sessions and Work here.** A member can host a session at a café, and other members can join it and take a seat. Work here is a session of your own: you choose a café, an end time, a type and whether it’s “Open to others”, and you can stay up to 8 hours, never past closing time. When you started is shown to you alone; other members, those at your table included, see only the end. With “Open to others” on, your session’s card shows your name, photo and headline, the café, the type and the end, and members can join you. With it off, or with either Privacy switch off, only you, and anyone who already took a seat in it, see the session, and you show at the café according to your Privacy switches. When you host a session, you’re always shown as its host. “Can’t make it” frees your seat, and the host is told with your name, or “Someone” when your Privacy switches hide you. “Running late” tells the host the same way, once per session (even if you leave and join again), from 30 minutes before the start until the end; Kerja keeps nothing but the queued notification, and sends no time or place. People on either side of a block never see each other in a session, and a banned member’s sessions and seats are hidden from everyone. If a host deletes their account, their sessions go with everyone’s seats.

**The waitlist.** When a session is full, a Work here open to others included, you can join its waitlist. Kerja keeps your place, when you joined and when a seat was held for you, until the session ends, or until you leave the waitlist, take a seat, or a block or a ban removes you. Only you see your place. When a seat opens, the first in line is told “A seat opened at” the café, and the seat is held for them for 15 minutes, then offered to the next. A session whose host chose “Ask to join” has no waitlist: when a host switches a session to “Ask to join”, its waitlist is deleted, and any seat held for someone on it is freed.

**Asks to join.** At a session whose host chose “Ask to join”, you can ask to join. Kerja keeps your ask (the session, who asked and when). You and the session’s host see it: the host sees your name, photo and headline and when you asked, even if you turned off “Show me in who’s here”; people on either side of a block with the host, and banned members, don’t. The host can accept or decline it, on the session page or in the Inbox’s Requests. You can have up to 3 waiting asks at sessions that haven’t ended. You can’t ask in a session’s last 30 minutes, and from then on the host no longer sees a waiting ask and can’t accept it. If the host accepts, you get a seat and the ask is deleted. If the host declines, Kerja also keeps when they declined, you see “Not this time.”, and you can’t take it back or ask again. Otherwise you can take back a waiting ask with “Take back”, which deletes it. Every ask at a session is deleted when the session ends or is cancelled, and an ask is also deleted when you or the host blocks the other, when either of you is banned, or when either account is deleted. The host’s notification says only “Maya wants to join your Focus session”: the member’s name and the session’s type, never a café, a time or any message text. You aren’t notified of the host’s answer; Kerja shows it the next time you open the session. While a session holds an ask, waiting or declined, who can join it stays as it is and can’t be changed.

**After a session.** When you check out, Kerja lists the people at your table, following the café’s rules (the same Privacy switches). Say hi fills in a note you can change, and nothing is sent until you tap Send. Kerja then asks two optional questions, “How was the Wi-Fi?” and “Sockets?”. When you answer both, Kerja stores the two answers with your account, the session and the café: one set of answers per session, which you can’t read or change afterwards. Other members only ever see a café’s most common answers, once 5 members have answered within 90 days, never who answered or how many. Café details we add ourselves show at once. Your answers are deleted after 90 days, or with your account.

**Worked together.** If you and another member were both shown by name at the same table in the last 90 days, each of you sees “Worked together” on the other’s profile; nobody else does. It’s worked out from seats, which are kept 90 days, and never counts across a block or a ban. **People you’ve worked with** lists the members you sat with at a table, by the day, never the time; not those who hide from “who’s here”. Sessions you shared while either of you had blocked the other never count. Sessions before or after a block still do. It also never lists those who hide their café, nor anyone blocked or banned now; at most 50, the newest day first; only you see your list. **Blocked periods:** when you block someone and later unblock them, Kerja keeps when the block started and ended, so that “Worked together”, the people you met and “People you’ve worked with” never count a table from while it lasted. Tables before and after it still count. Nobody sees it. It’s deleted with either account, and once no table it could hide is left (about 90 days at most).

**Your work stats and streak.** Your work stats (on Me and in My sessions) are worked out from your own seats this month, and your streak from your own seats over up to 12 weeks. Nothing new is kept, and only you see them.

**Cafés.** A café can show a partner line or a laptop rule. These are facts about cafés, not about people, and Kerja shares nothing about members with partner cafés.

**Chats.** You can message a member once one of you has accepted the other’s request. A chat and its messages are visible only to its two members, and to us only through a report (see Reports and bans). Each member’s read position is kept for that member only, to show what’s unread, and is never shown to anyone. **Delivered:** when a message reaches the other person’s phone (with Kerja open, or when it next opens), their app tells Kerja which messages arrived. Kerja keeps that a message was delivered, without a time, and shows “Delivered” to the sender the next time the sender’s chat loads; it’s never sent to the sender live. Only the recipient’s own app can mark a message delivered, never across a block or a ban, and a notification never counts. **Read:** “Show when I’ve read messages” is off by default. If you turn it on, Kerja keeps, for each chat, when you first read the other person’s latest message. “Read” with that time shows only while you both have it on; a time kept while only you had it on shows once the other person turns it on too. Turning it off deletes those times; a time also goes with the message it names, and with your account. It never applies to requests. Kerja has no typing indicator and no online status. “Not now” on a request is private: the sender is never told, and the request keeps showing as “Pending” to them. If either of you blocks the other, the chat disappears for both of you at once, and unblocking doesn’t bring it back; connecting again does, with its earlier messages. If a member is banned, their chats are hidden from everyone.

**Who can send you requests.** In Privacy, you choose who can send you requests: Everyone, People I’ve worked with (members you shared a table with in the last 90 days, both shown by name), or Nobody new. If someone can’t send you a request, they’re told only that it can’t be sent now. Requests already waiting stay.

**Words we refuse.** Our server refuses names, headlines, Instagram usernames, profile sections, request notes and the other texts members write when they contain words on our list (slurs, sexual insults, profanity, sexual solicitation, harassment, hate, scams and illegal gambling). Chat messages follow the same list, except ordinary swear words. A refused text is neither stored nor logged. If the name Apple or Google gives us is refused, we don’t store it, and you type your own.

**Reports and bans.** When you report someone, we keep the report: the reason, your note, whether you blocked them and the time, and, to know who was reported, their name and headline at that moment and scrambled fingerprints (SHA-256) of their email addresses and Apple or Google sign-in identifiers. When you report someone from a chat, our server also keeps a copy of the message you reported, or of that member’s last 10 messages in the chat, with their times; never your own messages. We keep a report and its copies even if either account is deleted, and delete them one year after we close the report. Each report sends us an email alert through Resend with the report’s number, reason and time, whether the reporter also blocked the person, and which of our two databases it came from. It never contains names or the note. To stop reports being misused, each time you send one our server keeps a scrambled form (a keyed hash) of your account ID and of your IP address, with the time, for up to 25 hours, and refuses more than 10 reports an hour from one account or more than 30 from one connection. If a report is refused for this, a block you asked for with it still happens. If we ban a member, we keep the same fingerprints for as long as the ban lasts, even after their account is deleted, so that the same email address or Apple or Google sign-in can’t open a new account. A banned member is hidden from everyone. A banned or deleted account can read and write nothing, even while its sign-in is still valid (up to an hour), except its own notification tokens, so that signing out still works, and what anyone can see before signing in.

**Notifications.** Kerja never asks at launch, and works without them. If you turn them on in Kerja, your phone gives Kerja a notification token, a code that lets us reach that phone, stored with the sign-in session it belongs to. A token is never used once its session has ended, including when our sign-in service has signed that phone out, and we delete it within a day; otherwise we keep it until you sign out or delete your account. An account keeps at most 10 phones: when a new phone registers, tokens whose session has ended go first, then the least recently used. Kerja’s server sends these notifications: “Maya wants to connect”, “Maya accepted your request” and “New message from Maya”; to a session’s host, “Maya joined your session at Crate Café” (grouped when several join; members hidden by their Privacy switches are counted, never named), “Maya can’t make it to your session at Crate Café” and “Maya is running late for your session at Crate Café” (“Someone” when the member’s Privacy switches hide them); to those who joined, “Maya cancelled the session at Crate Café”; to everyone holding a seat who joined at least 15 minutes before, the host included, “Focus at Crate Café starts in 15 minutes”; to the first on a waitlist, “A seat opened at Crate Café”; and to a session’s host, “Maya wants to join your Focus session”. On Android, session notifications have their own “Sessions” channel. They never contain the text of a message or a note. Messages are grouped: at most one notification per chat every 5 minutes, and none once you’ve read the chat. Nothing is sent between members who have blocked each other, or to or about a banned member, and a notification goes to at most your 4 most recently used phones. To send them, our server keeps a queue (see the table above): a notification not sent within 15 minutes is never sent, and every entry is deleted within 25 hours. Notifications go through Expo, which passes them to Apple or Google; we keep Expo’s answers (counts and error codes, never content) for 6 hours. Only Kerja’s server can send them: Expo refuses any request without Kerja’s private key. You can turn them off at any time in your phone’s settings.

**Usage statistics.** Nothing is sent to PostHog before you sign in. Besides one event when you sign in (with the sign-in method and whether the account is new), the app sends seven events to PostHog: each time the app opens or you come back to it, when you take a seat in a session, when you confirm you’re there (“I’m here” or “Start working”), when you check out, when you send a request, when you accept a request, and when you send a message. Besides what PostHog’s code adds to every event (see Service providers), each carries only the event’s name, a random install ID that PostHog makes on your phone while you’re signed in and deletes when you sign out or delete your account, so it’s never shared between accounts, and whether it comes from the live or the test version of the app: never your account, a session, a café, a request, a chat or another member, and never a position. PostHog doesn’t look up where an IP address is, keeps no profile of you, and its automatic events are off.

**Sign-in codes.** We keep only a scrambled form of each code, and it expires after one hour. There are no passwords.

**Beta applications.** The Join the beta page on trykerja.com has a short form. When you tap Send application, the page sends what you typed to our database in Singapore. The page keeps nothing in your browser, though your browser’s own autofill may remember what you typed. We keep one application per email address: if you apply again with the same address, the first stays as it was. We read applications in our database. For each new application, Resend sends us a short alert with the application’s number, which fits you best, how long you’re in Canggu, the page’s language, the time and which of our two databases it came from; never your name, email address, profile link or what you wrote. For a new application, Resend also sends you one email from hello@mail.trykerja.com, in the page’s language, to say we got it. It greets you by your first name if that name has only letters, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes, and contains nothing else you typed. Applying again with the same address sends no second email, and on a day when the form has already sent many emails, or when we can’t check how many, it sends neither email; your application is kept all the same. We can also switch off the email to you. When we invite you, we write to you ourselves from hello@trykerja.com. To stop the form being misused, each time it’s sent and passes its checks, our server keeps a scrambled form (a keyed hash) of the sender’s IP address and of the email address typed, with the time, for up to 25 hours, and refuses more than 5 sendings an hour from one connection or 3 a day for one email address. We can’t turn these back into an address, though we could check a guess against them. Your IP address is never stored with your application. Each application is deleted automatically 6 months after it was sent, or sooner if you ask us at hello@trykerja.com. Deleting a Kerja account doesn’t delete a beta application; ask us, or it goes after 6 months. You must be 18 or older to apply.

**Website statistics.** trykerja.com counts visits with Umami Cloud, a service of Umami Software, Inc., without cookies. Umami’s counting code is served from trykerja.com itself. Once a page has loaded, your browser sends Umami the page’s address, without anything after ? or #, and its title; the site you came from, as your browser gives it (usually only its domain; nothing when you move between our pages); your screen size; and your browser’s language. A few taps each send one event, with its name and at most two short words saying which button or which language: Join the beta, the language switch, the menu, the Instagram link and the TestFlight links. Once the beta form has an answer, one event says whether it went through and, if not, gives one word for why (for example “invalid” or “offline”); never anything you typed. Like every request, these also carry your IP address and your browser’s User-Agent. From them, Umami works out your browser, system and type of device, your country, region and city, and a visitor code: a scrambled form (a hash) of the website, your IP address, your User-Agent and a secret that changes every month, which lets it count visitors without cookies. Umami doesn’t keep your IP address in the statistics, though Umami and its providers may keep IP addresses in their own security and operations logs. In Umami, we see totals and, for each visitor code, its visits, with their pages, taps, times, device, browser and place; never a name, an email address or an account. We never link the statistics to the app or to beta applications. Umami keeps the statistics in its EU region, for 6 months; copies in its backups are deleted on its own backup cycle. Nothing is sent if your browser sends Do Not Track (not every browser offers it), or once you tap “Don’t count my visits” on Our promise: your browser then keeps one setting for trykerja.com (umami.disabled) to remember your choice, and “Count them again” removes it. If your browser clears the site’s data (Safari can after 7 days without a visit; a private window does when it closes), tap it again.

**What we don’t collect.** No contacts, access to your photo library, camera (except to take your profile photo, if you choose to), microphone, advertising ID or cross-app tracking.

**Legal bases (EU and UK):** providing the service you asked for (account, profile, photo, check-ins, sessions, seats, waitlist places and asks to join, areas, requests, chats with their delivered and read states, café answers), and handling the beta application you sent us; our legitimate interests (keeping members safe through the word filter, reports, bans and blocked periods; security, including the limits on the beta form and on reports; crash reports; statistics, the website’s included; answering your emails, waitlist emails included); your consent (location, camera and notification permissions); and recording your acceptance of our terms.

## What other members see

- **Your profile, at any time:** signed-in members can see your name, photo, headline, what you do, what you’re looking for, your city and either the date you’re there until or, in its place, that you live there (shown as “Lives in” and your city), your portfolio link and your Instagram username, what you’re building, what you need right now, what you can help with, what you’re open to and your answers to questions. They don’t see when your profile was created or last updated. Their apps also receive your random member ID and three account flags (LinkedIn verified, work email verified and Pro); none of these is shown, and the flags are always off for now. Kerja doesn’t check that an Instagram username belongs to the person who entered it. In People, members are listed until their date has passed; a member who lives there has no date, and stays listed until they choose one instead.
- **Working at a café:** while you’re working at a café, members see you there: on the map as your photo or initial (up to three people per café, then a number), and in the café’s “Who’s here” with your name, headline, what you do and what you’re looking for. A member’s app receives up to 50 people listed at each café it shows, and shows 8: good fits first, worked out on their phone, then by name; everyone is counted. With “Show me in who’s here” off, you count at the café as a blank circle, and your name and photo are never sent. With “Show which café I’m at” off, you aren’t shown or counted at any café. Their apps receive when your time there ends, rounded up to the next quarter hour, and never when you arrived. This ends when you check out, at the end you chose or the session’s end (8 hours at most), or at the café’s closing time.
- **In a session:** every signed-in member sees a session’s host and the people who joined it, except people on either side of a block and banned members. Members hidden by “Show me in who’s here” are counted, never named, except to a host they asked to join (see Asks to join); a session’s host is always shown. Who was at a table, and who left it, only the host and the people who held a seat there see; everyone else sees who’s there now and how many seats are taken. Members never receive when another member joined, checked in or left; you see only your own seats’ times. Members don’t see when a session last changed. A session shows its type, whether it’s “Open” or “Ask to join”, and what its host will work on. For a session that was still to come or under way when this version came out, that’s its house rules, cut to 80 characters (or its type’s words if it had none); for a session made on an older version of the app since, its type’s words. This version of the app no longer shows house rules; older versions still do. Once a session has ended, only its host and the people who held a seat see it, with when it ended.
- **Live:** Members watching the map can see when you appear at, or leave, a café, a session or an area. Kerja keeps when you arrived, but never gives that time to other members.
- **Around an area:** if you say you’re around an area today, members see you around it, on the map, in People and on your profile, with your name, photo, headline, what you do, what you’re looking for and what you’re up for, plus a count of people around it. A member’s app receives up to 50 people listed per area, by name; everyone is counted. The same switches apply: with “Show which café I’m at” off, you can’t choose an area; with “Show me in who’s here” off, you’re counted but never listed. If you’re checked in at a café, you show only at the café. This ends when you tap “Stop for today”, or at midnight in Bali.
- **Worked together:** only you and the other member see “Worked together” on each other’s profiles (see Worked together above).
- **Counts:** before signing in, anyone who opens Kerja sees how many people are working at cafés in Canggu right now, only when there are 20 or more, rounded down to tens (for example “30+ people”), and how many sessions there are today. No names, and nothing about anyone in particular. Once signed in, the map’s line shows how many people are working only when there are 20 or more.
- **Your requests:** the person you send a request to sees who it’s from, the reason, your note and any café you suggested, in their Inbox and on your profile. Nobody else sees it. If they tap “Not now”, you aren’t told.
- **Your messages:** only the member you’re chatting with sees them, with when you sent them, and “Sent” or “Delivered”. They see when you’ve read their messages only while you both have “Show when I’ve read messages” on, and never when you’re typing or whether you’re online. If you delete your account, they keep only the messages they sent you, under “This member left Kerja”, without your name or photo.
- **Your next city and its date:** only members you’re connected with, and only while “Share my next city and dates” is on.
- **Blocks:** you and the person you block see nothing of each other, and your chat disappears for both of you. Only you see the people you’ve blocked, with the date, in Blocked members.
- **Only you see** your email, your check-in and session history, your seats’ times, your work stats, your streak, your waitlist places, your asks to join (which the session’s host also sees), your “People you’ve worked with” list, your requests’ history, where you’ve read up to in each chat, your privacy choices and your account details.
- **Get directions** opens Google Maps with the café’s location, and **Open in Instagram** opens Instagram; their own policies apply there.

## Your choices and rights

You can change your profile, photo and privacy choices, or delete your account and everything with it, from inside the app.

- **Your profile and photo:** Me → Edit profile, or tap your photo. You can change or remove your photo at any time.
- **Privacy:** Me → Settings → Privacy, to hide which café you’re at or the area you’re around, leave “Who’s here”, stop sharing your next city, choose who can send you requests, turn “Show when I’ve read messages” on or off, and see the people you’ve blocked.
- **Your area for the day:** tap your area at the bottom of the map, then “Stop for today”.
- **Notifications:** Me → Settings → Notifications, or your phone’s settings.
- **Delete your account:** Me → Settings → Delete account. Your account, profile, photos, privacy choices, blocks and blocked periods, check-ins, the sessions you hosted (with everyone’s seats in them), your seats, waitlist places, asks to join and café answers, area, requests, every message you sent, whether messages in your chats were delivered, your read positions and read times, acceptance of the terms, notification tokens and sign-in sessions are removed from our database and photo storage at once. In each of your chats, the other member keeps only the messages they sent, read-only, under “This member left Kerja”, with no name or photo of you; a chat where they wrote nothing is deleted at once. Reports, with any copies of reported messages, and ban fingerprints are kept for safety, as described above. Service logs expire within 7 days; crash reports and statistics aren’t linked to your account and expire as above.
- **Log out:** Me → Settings → Log out (Sign out, in older versions of the app). This signs you out on all your devices, removes their notification tokens, and clears the suggestions, recent searches, photo step mark and photos your phone keeps.
- **Apple or Google sign-in:** you can also remove Kerja’s access in your Apple ID or Google account settings.
- **Location:** allow or refuse it in your phone’s settings at any time. Working at a café and “I’m here” need it, with precise location on; everything else works without it.
- **Camera:** allow or refuse it in your phone’s settings at any time. Only taking your profile photo with the camera needs it; you can choose a photo from your library instead.
- **Your beta application:** to change or withdraw it, email hello@trykerja.com from the address you applied with, and we’ll correct or delete it.
- **Website statistics:** tap “Don’t count my visits” on Our promise, in each browser you use, or turn on your browser’s Do Not Track. The statistics hold no name or email address, so we can’t tell which visits are yours.
- **Access, correction, a copy, objection or restriction:** email hello@trykerja.com. We answer within one month.
- **Complaints:** you can complain to a data protection authority, for example the one in the country where you live.
